Voluntary Control Terrain

Each hill is a documented case of people changing a feature of their own body. Height is how far that change moves the feature from the healthy-adult average, in standard deviations. The ground plots how certain the evidence is against how many people can do it.

Method

Strength = size of the change ÷ the population SD of that feature in healthy adults at rest. Where a source reports a standardized effect size (d or g), that is used. Elevation is drawn on a log scale so a 30 SD spike and a 0.3 SD rise both stay readable.

Certainty rates whether the control exists, not whether it helps clinically. High: objectively measured, replicated by independent groups or meta-analysis, mechanism understood. Moderate: objective, controlled, but small samples or mostly one group. Low: one study, a handful of people, or not peer-reviewed. Not supported: tested and not shown.

Who can do it: None · Single cases (≤3 people) · Adepts (a small expert group, rate unmeasured) · Rare (<5%, measured) · Minority (5–50%, measured) · Most, with training (group studies).
